# research-papers

Citations and short independent briefs of my published legal research. Copyright in the published works is assigned to the publishers, so this repo carries citations and briefs in my own words - never the published full text.

## Published

1. **Police Surveillance Technologies and Privacy Protection in India: A Constitutional and Institutional Analysis of Facial Recognition Systems Post-Puttaswamy Judgement**
   Indian Journal of Law and Legal Research, Vol VII, Issue VI, ISSN 2582-8878 (certificate dated 18 Jan 2026).
   Examines India's National Automated Facial Recognition System through the Puttaswamy proportionality test, questions its statutory basis, safeguards and oversight, and proposes a moratorium or rights-based legislation.
   Journal page: https://www.ijllr.com/post/police-surveillance-technologies-and-privacy-protection-in-india-a-constitutional-and-institutional

2. **Between Blocking and Freedom: Analyzing the Karnataka High Court's Ruling in X Corp v. Union of India, 2025** (case comment)
   IILS Law Review, Vol 12, Issue 2 (2026), ISSN 2320-5318, pp. 137-144.
   Examines takedown powers, the Sahyog Portal and the constitutional and procedural safeguards in the High Court's 24 September 2025 ruling.

3. **Soft Law as an Instrument of Global Governance: Creation of Norms Without Coercion**
   International Journal of Legal Research and Governance, Vol 6, Issue 4 (Jul-Sep 2026), ISSN 2394-7829, pp. 10-18.
   Examines how declarations, standards and guidelines create international norms without coercion, and the accountability questions that follow.
   Journal page: https://ijlrg.lawmantra.co.in

4. **TWAIL and Digital Governance: Power, Data Colonialism, and Regulatory Sovereignty** (book chapter)
   In *Regulation, Policy and Power: Law in a Rapidly Transforming World*, Juris Cognita International Publications x Amity Law School Punjab, ISBN 978-81-995401-8-7.
   Applies Third World Approaches to International Law to data colonialism and soft-law standard-setting, and reads India's localisation and digital public infrastructure as regulatory sovereignty.
